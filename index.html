      What happened

        Rules card

        Back to the game

        Start with three rules

        1. Count your steps. Walk any route you like, but never onto a square you have already crossed on this page (your trail). Each page allows a number of steps; past it, every 4th step is a skull: −1 Health. Most of the Health a run loses goes to this track, not to enemies, so look at the exit before you walk.
        2. Play your hand. Walk into an enemy and it flips blind number cards, one at a time. Whenever it is above you, add cards from your hand to be level, or stop and lose. Or pay 4 Energy to let it pass.
        3. Step into the hole (or the stair) to go on: the next circle, grid or page. A boss ends each chapter.

        Your first game is the Pilgrim, the plain rules; the other character cards open in the Ledger (below). A hand card is worth about a Health point: spend the smallest that works, and walk round what your hand can’t cover. Everything else is explained the first time you meet it, and below.

        Symbols

        YouStart in the corner the chapter's first page says (Inferno: the top corner at the fold of page 21).

        WallYou can’t cross it. Violet: printed on a clear sheet.

        DoorOnly where two routes rejoin. Cross it only the way it opens (the chevron). Every other passage is open both ways.

        Merge markA junction where routes rejoin: leave it only by an arrow. It says what the doors on the routes joining it say, with fewer symbols.

        Your trailThe squares you have crossed on this page. Never step onto one again. It is wiped when you leave the page.

        HoleOne per circle, in a corner. Drops you onto the same corner of the next circle.

        Enemy ①②③A court card dealt face up when the page is set up. It starts at the page’s number (3 on the first circles, 10 on the last) and flips blind number cards: jack 1, queen 2, king 3, a Fury 1 after a die. A loss costs 2 damage (jack, queen) or 3 (king, Fury).

        Hidden enemyFace down until you meet it. The plain one always is; the page header hides more on deeper circles, and any enemy standing in the dark is hidden too.

        EliteTwo court cards. It fights as the stronger, it flips one card more and hits 1 harder. Beat it and take its two highest cards, then draw your hand back up to 5.

        “?” markA walk stops on it. Flip a number card: ♥ +1/+2 Health, ♦ +2/+4 Energy, ♠ 1/2 damage, ♣ −2/−4 Energy (2–5 small, 6–10 big).

        GateStrength 2 walks through. Otherwise pay 5 Energy, or take 2 damage. It never takes a card and never stops your walk.

        Toll4 Energy as you pass. It is never paid in Health: with less than 4 Energy you cannot use that way, so walk round.

        Fountain+1 Health and either +3 Energy or a hand card. One per circle.

        Chest+2 Health and +2 Energy. Meal: +3 Health. Flask: +3 Energy.

        Rest+3 Health, for 4 steps.

        Altar3 Energy for a hand card, or 4 Energy for +1 strength (at most 3).

        The bossesCerberus, Lucifer, the Wall of Fire and Saint Peter each cover 3 by 3 squares over the hole, so every way leads to him. Each one’s rules are in the list “The bosses” below.

        Everything else

        You and your character card

        Everyone starts with a hand of 5 cards (number cards, 2–10), strength at most 3. Then pick a character card: a few changed starting numbers (the Pilgrim has 10 Health, 12 Energy, most 15, strength 0) and nothing else. Health at 0 ends your run.

          Setting up a page

          When you arrive on a page, roll two dice for the clear maze sheet (the first picks it; face up on 1–3 of the second, face down on 4–6) and two for the room sheet; lay both with their stars on the exit corner. Whatever is printed on the page wins, then the maze sheet, then the room sheet. On circles 6, 7 and 8 roll one more die, the smoke die: 1–2 the page allows 4 steps fewer, 3–4 nothing, 5–6 four more. Deal a court card face up at each numbered enemy the page header allows. Draw your hand back up to 5 (and again after every win). Leave the page and the sheets come off, your trail with them, and every court card goes back in the court pile.

          Walking

          You walk by counting squares, turning as you like. A walk goes on until there is something to choose: a junction, a room, a mark, an enemy in the way, a dotted hole, the hole. Tick a box on the page’s step track per square; past the allowance every 4th box is a skull (−1 Health). Never step onto a square you have already crossed on this page. Mark your trail as you go. Follow that and the doors and you can never get stuck: every way leads on to the hole, and there is always a way with no enemy, mark, gate or toll on it. Bubble rides and ice slides are free steps.

          Crossroads and signposts

          Every maze sheet has two crossroads: a fast way (a few squares, guarded by a gate, a toll or a hidden enemy), a rich way (a few squares longer, through the room sheet’s rooms) and a safe way (the longest, with nothing on it). Other forks are signposted: a reward on the longer way, a risk or a price on the shorter. Choose with your hand and your steps in mind.

          The fight

          One fight, step by step

            The details. Move into an enemy: fight, or pay 4 Energy to let it pass. Pay before it flips its first card; you can never pay a shortfall in Health, so without 4 Energy you fight or walk round. Its total starts at the page’s number; it flips number cards one at a time. After each, if it is above you (hand cards you have played, plus strength, plus 1 if you started from the dark), add hand cards until you are level, or stop: you lose and take its damage. Level after its last card is a win: you draw back up to 5 (an elite also gives its two highest cards, and your lowest goes if you hold more than 5). The cards you played are replaced from the pile, so your hand is full again but not always stronger. After a loss its total is known: fight again with cards that beat it, pay 4 Energy, or walk round. Against a boss who fights you may always swing blind instead of committing cards: the top number card counts for you. Some bosses allow an Energy flip; see the boss list.

            The bosses

              Chapter 1, Inferno: the circles and their terrain

                Chapter 2, Purgatory: the terraces

                Each terrace is two 9 by 9 grids, the lower first. The hole of the lower grid lands you on the same corner of the upper, whose hole is the stair. One step track covers both. The Lightening: at the stair, every 4 steps you did not use heal 1 Health, at most 2; what you cannot take yet is banked (up to 2) for a later stair. The last page is the Wall of Fire on one 13 by 13 grid: each round he flips two cards and you must match their sum; a blind swing is the whole round; three rounds won beat him.

                  Chapter 3, Paradise: the spheres

                  Each sphere prints four 7 by 7 doors, each with a reward: Mercy (Health), Plenty (Energy), Wisdom (a hand card), Haste (steps on the next page). Choose one, then roll its tile (two dice) and walk it; the reward is yours at its hole, and the other three stay closed. The door you took on the last page is shut on this one. The last page is Saint Peter (see the list of bosses).

                    Chapters, blessings and ways to play

                    Normal plays chapters 1, 2 and 3, one chapter a sitting. Winning a chapter refills your Health and Energy, gives you a new deck (your hand and strength stay) and blessings (three after Inferno, one after Purgatory), each picked from three, that last the run and stack: Tough (+3 most Health), Swift (+3 steps a page, +2 in Paradise) or Strong (+1 strength, most 3, and +3 in every fight). Hard starts at chapter 2 and Brutal at chapter 3, at the base values of your character card, with no blessings and no bonus. Each chapter won is a tick in the Ledger; Hard opens when you have won chapter 1, Brutal when you have won chapter 2.

                    Try any chapter, character or way to play at once: open More and choose Practice run… Every character and every way to play (Normal, Hard, Brutal) is open, with every Heat burden, and there is no Virgil’s bonus. Nothing is written in the Ledger.

                    The book

                    • An A5 book of 40 pages. Inferno runs down the right-hand pages 21 to 37: one 13 by 13 grid per page, the circle’s lines from Dante beneath it, and its header of dials.
                    • Every circle has one hole, in a corner. It drops you onto the same corner of the next circle, two pages on. There is no way back up.
                    • Purgatory climbs back up the left-hand pages 38 to 20; Paradise goes up the right-hand pages 19 to 1, with its lines and the legend of the doors on the even page facing each sphere. The last page you reach is the first page of the book.

                    Between runs: Virgil’s bonus and the Pilgrim’s Ledger (inside back cover)

                    Virgil’s bonus. Your first run is the plain game. After a run you lose, Virgil offers you one small bonus for the next run: roll a die for each of three places and read the table (roll again for a face you already have); take one. If you fell before circle 5, he has only two places; a run lost before circle 3 earns no new bonus. The bonus lasts one run. A win clears the bonus, so the next run is plain again. A run you give up before circle 3 counts for nothing.

                    DieBonus

                    The Pilgrim’s Ledger. A list of boxes. Tick a box the first time it happens, in any run, won or lost (a Hard or Brutal run ticks only the boxes of the chapters it plays). Some boxes open a character card; the others are your record. The Ledger never makes you stronger: it only opens choices. You start with the Pilgrim.

                      Heat: burdens for a better record (optional)

                        Win

                        Get past the boss of each chapter and go through his hole: Lucifer, the Wall of Fire, Saint Peter. Count your steps: fewer is better.

                        On your phone or tablet

                        Commedia runs entirely in your browser: no account, nothing is sent anywhere, and once this page has loaded it needs no connection. A run you leave is kept on this device, in this browser; clearing the browser’s site data erases it, and the Ledger with it.

                        To keep it on this device and play offline: on an iPhone or iPad, Share, then Add to Home Screen; on Android, the browser menu, then Install app (or Add to Home screen). After that it opens like an app, with no connection.

                        On a computer you can also download it as one file that plays with no internet: Commedia.zip. Unzip it and open Commedia.html. (A phone or tablet cannot open a loose file: use Add to Home Screen above.)
